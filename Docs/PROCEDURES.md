# Field procedures in the game

Reference: US Army **FM 19-20, Law Enforcement Investigations**, Part Three "Obtaining and Recording Physical Evidence"
(Chapters 7-11). The game follows these procedures where they concern a shooting scene. Mechanics are written in our
own words; nothing is quoted into game text.

## Implemented

| Procedure (chapter) | How the game uses it | Keys |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Photograph and locate evidence before touching or moving it (Ch 10; also Ch 7, 8) | Nothing can be packaged until it has been marked and photographed in place | 1, then P |
| Never probe for a bullet with metal tools; take out the section of wall with the bullet in it (Ch 9) | Recovering a bullet cuts out the section, so its rifling marks survive for the lab | 6 |
| Never mark bullets or cartridge cases; put them in a padded, sealed container and mark the container (Ch 9) | Packaging text follows this; the container is marked, never the item | P |
| Mark firearms on the frame, slide/cylinder and barrel; mark the magazine on its base (Ch 9) | Packaging a firearm records these marks | P |
| Unload firearms before sending them to the lab; never clean them (Ch 9) | A firearm can't be packaged until it has been made safe | 0, then P |
| Revolver: mark the chamber under the firing pin and diagram the cylinder before opening it (Ch 9) | Recording the condition first (9) marks the chamber; opening it first loses the order of the chambers for good | 9, then 0 |
| Lab tests: class characteristics of bullets and cases, test-fire comparison, function test (Ch 9) | Crime-lab requests: limited per case, results after a wait; bullets and cases are matched only against weapons also sent in | L |
| Bullet through glass: clean hole on the entry side, cone-shaped crater on the exit side (Ch 10) | Edge examination states which face the crater opens toward | 9 on glass |
| Sideways angle through glass: little chipping on the side the bullet came from, heavy chipping opposite; short radial cracks on that side at the entry (Ch 10) | Edge examination says which side the bullet came in from, and whether straight on, moderate or steep | 9 on glass |
| Firing order: a new crack stops where it meets an existing crack (Ch 10) | Glass crack pattern; edge examination tells the player how to read it | 9 on glass |

## Considered for later cases

| Procedure (chapter) | Why not yet | Fits in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Muzzle-to-target distance from powder residue on clothing: usually no visible pattern beyond about 75 cm, loose particles up to about 2.4 m (Ch 9) | Needs a garment at the scene; the Kitchen case has none | A case with a victim's jacket or a cushion as the target |
| Gunshot primer residue on hands (Ch 9) | Shows someone fired or handled a gun, not which gun; nobody is at the scene to swab | A case with a suspect present |
| Radial and concentric fractures, the 4-R rule for which side a blow came from (Ch 10) | Needs a pane broken by a blow (e.g. a staged break-in) | A case with a broken door or window pane |
| Heat fractures (wavy, no point of impact) (Ch 10) | Needs a fire scene | Later |
| Safety glass: concentric cracks on the impact side, radial on the far side (Ch 10) | Needs a vehicle scene | A car-shooting case |
| Latent fingerprints: photograph before lifting; don't powder a firearm that is going to the firearms lab (Ch 7) | Needs a fingerprint identification system | Phase 5 content |
| Casts of footwear and tyre impressions (Ch 8) | Needs outdoor scenes | Exterior cases |
| Trace evidence: hairs, fibres, bloodstain shape and age, tool marks (Ch 11) | Separate evidence families from shooting reconstruction | Later, case by case |
